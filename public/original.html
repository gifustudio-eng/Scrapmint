Why We Exist

About
Scrapmint

Indonesia generates 70 million tons of municipal waste per year — that's roughly 190,000 tons every single day. One of the highest volumes in Southeast Asia.

Most waste ends up in landfills. Open burning is common. Rivers carry plastic to the sea. Indonesia is the world's third-largest ocean plastic polluter — not because Indonesians don't care, but because the systems needed to handle this scale simply don't exist yet.

Standard solutions won't be enough. The scale demands creative, systemic thinking. New infrastructure. New models. New approaches — designed specifically for Indonesia's geography, economy, and density.

We're working on it.
Vision
"To build a generation of young innovators who turn scrap into sustainable materials and create measurable environmental impact."
Mission
We solve the problem of waste by raising awareness and bringing innovative solutions to stakeholders — turning waste into worth through infrastructure that is practical, scalable, and built for Indonesia's reality.
03 / System Framing

The Gap Is
Infrastructure.

Indonesia doesn't have a recycling problem. It has a systems problem. Collection is fragmented. Processing is informal. Enforcement is inconsistent. No single actor can close this loop alone — which is why Scrapmint builds the connective tissue.

01
Infrastructure Gap

Indonesia generates 70 million tons of municipal solid waste per year — roughly 190,000 tons every single day. Existing collection and sorting infrastructure covers less than a third of that volume. Material Recovery Facilities are sparse, under-resourced, and disconnected from downstream markets.

02
Enforcement Gap

Regulations exist. Implementation doesn't. Extended Producer Responsibility frameworks remain paper policies without operational systems to back them. The compliance infrastructure simply isn't there — and without it, the 14% formal recycling rate cannot move.

03
Scale Gap

Community programs work at the block level. Industry operates at national scale. Nothing bridges them. Scrapmint is built specifically to operate at the layer between — turning local execution into systemic coverage that can be replicated across every city in the archipelago.

04 / Capabilities

What
Scrapmint
Does.

01
Infrastructure Design

We map waste flows, identify collection gaps, and design physical systems — sorting hubs, drop points, transfer stations — tailored to the density and material profile of each zone. Every system is built for long-term operability, not just proof-of-concept.

02
Waste Processing & Recovery

We operate or co-develop processing capacity for plastics, organics, and mixed materials — linking recovered output to industrial offtakers and circular product streams. From Material Recovery Facilities to insect-based protein, we close every loop we open.

03
Circular Economy Projects

We develop project-based interventions with measurable material impact — from innovative recycling with start-ups (Ecobricks, Waste Bench, Foodcycle) to mall-wide waste diversion programs that redirect up to 80% of waste away from landfill.

04
Institutional Partnerships

We work with malls, corporates, and development institutions to design compliance-ready, fundable waste programs — from scoping through to implementation and reporting. Our #MulaiDariSini campaign has already committed major malls to divert waste from landfill.

Scrap Into

We engaged local and international start-ups to recycle and upcycle waste into new products or structures — demonstrating that waste, if properly segregated, is a valuable resource, not a problem to be buried.

Ecobricks

07 / Process

How We
Build.

01
Phase One
Diagnose

Material flow analysis and on-site audit — quantifying waste volumes, mapping informal haulers, and identifying the exact infrastructure gaps that Scrapmint is positioned to close.

02
Phase Two
Design

System architecture — collection routing, MRF configuration, market linkages — with a proven business model and measurable impact metrics built in from day one.

03
Phase Three
Implement

Physical infrastructure stands up. Mall tenants educated. Community and institutional partners onboarded. Field operations run by a hands-on youth team that gets into the facility themselves.

04
Phase Four
Scale

Document what works, package for replication, expand — deeper into the same geography or horizontally across new cities — without losing the operational discipline that makes results real.
